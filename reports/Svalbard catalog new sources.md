# Cheap ZIMs and Finnish data fill Svalbard's gaps

Svalbard's 227-recipe catalog already covers most of what similar projects ship, so the biggest wins now are small. The main gaps are about fifteen Kiwix ZIMs, PDFs and Finnish open datasets that fit recipe shapes the catalog already has, and they total about 7 GB. The best value per GB is The Last Codex, the suomi.fi and SPEK preparedness guides, Be Ready UK, TED water and natural-disaster videos, a DevDocs bundle, English PhET, Simple English Wikipedia with English Wikivoyage, Gutenberg military and naval science, MML orthophotos and SYKE shoreline and depth layers, all CC BY or similar. Behind those are larger but valuable seams: structured datasets such as openFDA, GeoNames, FoodData Central, Kotus, Finlex and MML place names, which need new parsers because the `reference-static` builder only understands Fimea's XML. Offline routing and geocoding (BRouter, Photon) need a Java runtime. On the AI side, the llama.cpp upgrade gates the strongest new models, and multilingual embeddings mean rebuilding every semantic index. Six existing recipes have concrete defects that should be fixed before anything is added. `ready-gov` points at a dead filename series, `usda-nutrition` is really the USDA home-canning guide, the Gutenberg recipes fetch builds about eight months stale, llama-server is pinned to a build too old for November 2026 models, the Qwen3.8 pin has drifted, and vision projectors go unshipped. On vehicle repair, Svalbard covers fixing and troubleshooting through iFixit and the Mechanics and Bicycles Stack Exchanges, but it has no workshop manuals. The only openly redistributable additions are US Army generic automotive primers. Owner's manuals and Sheldon Brown are user-fetched, and the large CHARM/LEMON factory-manual archive is legally uncertain and should stay out of the catalog.

## How to read classes, sizes and the existing seams

Every item below carries one of three legal classes. **Class A (redistributable)** means public domain or an open licence, so the item is fine in a default preset and on a stick that is handed to someone else. Non-commercial (NC) Creative Commons items that Kiwix itself redistributes are marked **A-NC**, following the precedent of the existing `ted-*` recipes. **Class B (user-fetched)** means the item is free to download but has no redistribution grant. It suits an opt-in recipe that fetches from the official URL at apply time, a `*.local.yaml` build recipe like `recipes/content/faroutride.local.yaml`, or `svalbard import <file> --vault <path> --add`, with a "personal copy" flag on the recipe. **Class C** is legally uncertain or excluded, and it belongs in documentation at most.

Effort sizes follow the repo convention. They depend on four seams I checked in the repo. **`type: pdf` recipes already exist** (`recipes/content/who-basic-emergency-care.yaml`), so a single public PDF is an S. **The `vector-static` family takes SYKE shapefile ZIPs directly** (`recipes/maps/pohjavesialueet.yaml`), so another SYKE layer is an S. **`raster-tms` already pulls kapsi TMS tiles over a bbox** (`recipes/maps/mml-maastokartta.yaml`), so another kapsi tile layer is an S. **`reference-static` is hard-coded to Fimea's XML schema**: `host-cli/internal/builder/reference_static.go` rejects any `source_format` other than `xml` and unmarshals straight into `fimeaRegistry`. Every new structured dataset is therefore at least an M, because it needs its own parser. A generic table-mapping builder for CSV, TSV, JSON and XML would be an L that later turns each dataset into an S. The date resolver already accepts letter-suffixed Kiwix dates through the regex `(\d{4}-\d{2}[a-z]?)` in `host-cli/internal/resolver/resolver.go:35`, so the new `2026-11a` filenames need no code change.

## Pack-by-pack gaps

Unless stated otherwise, Kiwix sizes and dates come from the 2026-11-26 directory listings and OPDS feed cited above, and every Kiwix ZIM is an S.

### survival-medical: government guides, open drug data, and the PDF canon

The cheapest additions are Kiwix files: **Be Ready UK** (158M), **TED water** (832M), **natural-disaster** (683M), **medicine** (3.4G), **public-health** (2.2G) and **mental-health** (2.2G), and the three missing **Canadian Prepper** sets: bugoutroll 838M, bugoutconcepts 2.7G and preppingfood 2.0G. NOMAD already ships the Canadian Prepper sets in its survival tiers ([videos](https://download.kiwix.org/zim/videos/), [NOMAD manifest](https://github.com/Crosstalk-Solutions/project-nomad/blob/main/collections/kiwix-categories.json)). **Gutenberg LCC U** (864M) adds public-domain military field-craft. `wikipedia_en_knots_maxi` (13M) is a cheap complement but not a gap, because `zimgit-knots` already covers knots. The **US Army Publishing Directorate** ZIM (`zimit/armypubs_en_all_2024-12.zim`, 7.7 GiB / 8.2 GB, US government work) is the large "all doctrine" option. Its contents could not be inspected, and many equipment TMs are distribution-restricted, so treat it as an opt-in comprehensive tier ([zimit](https://download.kiwix.org/zim/zimit/)). The Skin of Color Society dermatology ZIM (1.41 GB) and the full `mdwiki_en_all` flavour (10.75 GB, compared with the 2.30 GB maxi in the catalog) are medical upgrades whose licences still need checking.

The most distinctive non-ZIM gap is **public-domain drug data**. openFDA drug labels come to about **1.8 GB zipped** (265,067 records in 13–14 JSON partitions; part 1 is 128 MB), CC0, at [api.fda.gov/download.json](https://api.fda.gov/download.json) ([part 1](https://download.open.fda.gov/drug/label/drug-label-0001-of-0013.json.zip)). NOMAD ships the same data as its 1,700 MB "FDA Drug Reference" ([NOMAD collections](https://github.com/Crosstalk-Solutions/project-nomad/tree/main/collections)). Turning it into an FTS table that complements `fimea` is an **L**: the data is multi-GB, it needs a schema design, and it should be refreshed yearly, because "drug information goes stale fastest" ([170 GB archive write-up](https://emresokullu.com/myblog/2026/05/06/doomsday-ready-170gb-offline-knowledge-archive.html)). NOMAD's `conditions.json`, `home_remedies.json` and `natural_remedies.json` are small public-domain lookups built from CDC, NIH, MedlinePlus and NCCIH "Herbs at a Glance". They are an M as a symptom→remedy table for search and AI. The hazmat trio is all public domain: the **NIOSH Pocket Guide** PDF ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/downloadable.html)), the **Emergency Response Guidebook 2024** ([PHMSA PDF](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/sites/phmsa.dot.gov/files/2024-04/ERG2024-Eng-Web-a.pdf)) and CAMEO Chemicals (a Windows/macOS desktop app, so it does not fit the catalog). Both PDFs blocked automated HEAD requests (403), so their URLs need a manual check before pinning.

The medical PDF canon from personal "doomsday" builds is **class B**: Hesperian's *Where There Is No Doctor* (2025, about 0.8 GB for the set, an NC "Open Copyright License" that asks for permission for some redistribution), MSF Medical Guidelines, the WHO International Medical Guide for Ships (likely CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, unverified), ICRC War Surgery and the IFRC First Aid Guidelines ([Hesperian FM PDF](https://hesperian.org/wp-content/uploads/pdf/en_wtnd_2025/en_wtnd_2025_fm.pdf), [MSF](https://medicalguidelines.msf.org/en), [WHO ship guide](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43814), [WikiProjectMed content list](https://mdwiki.org/wiki/WikiProjectMed:Internet-in-a-Box/Content)). They fit the existing `type: pdf` shape (WHO Basic Emergency Care is already there), but they should be opt-in. Hesperian's per-chapter filenames change between printings ([Hesperian warning](https://languages.hesperian.org/pages/uz/pdf.html)), so it is an M: a multi-file recipe with checksum pins. Kiwix has no Hesperian, WHO or MSF ZIM and no emergency-medicine ZIM to replace WikEM. Those gaps can only be filled from these class B PDFs.

The Kiwix additions are TED **gardening** (311M), **plants** (633M), **agriculture** (1.2G), **food** (2.2G) and **weather** (368M); **foss.cooking** (29M) and **Public Domain Recipes** (16M, Unlicense); and Stack Exchange **pets** (83M), **lifehacks** (47M), **coffee** (17M) and **vegetarianism** (9.3M). The catalog has DevDocs only for C, C++, Go, JavaScript, Python and Rust. A bundle of **bash, fish, git, jq, gcc, cmake, docker, nginx, kubernetes, nix, postgresql, sqlite, redis, lua, zig, typescript, node, html, css, http, markdown and man** comes to well under 60 MB. Every file is 0.3–28M and dated 2026-1x ([devdocs](https://download.kiwix.org/zim/devdocs/)). These are many S recipes of the same shape, ideally generated from a list. The larger zimit sites are **MDN** (560M, CC BY-SA 2.5), **docs.python.org** (3.6G), **lua.org** (362M), Jeff Erickson's *Algorithms* (493M, CC BY 4.0), **Open Data Structures** (111M), **Go by Example** (323K), **freeCodeCamp** (11M) and the **postmarketOS wiki** (29M). Corey Schafer's video courses (Python 2.4G, git 57M, SQL 22M) suit a comprehensive tier. Cheatography (10.9 GB, per-sheet licences) is too large and too unclear in licence to include.

### communications: software for radio and mesh, which the pack lacks entirely

The communications pack is reference-only today (Stack Exchange, LibreTexts, OpenWrt) and ships no radio software. Kiwix has no new radio ZIM. The gap is tools. **Pat** (Winlink email over radio, v1.0.1, MIT, 5–6M on Linux, macOS only as an amd64 pkg), **rtl_433** (25.12, GPL-2.0, small zips) and the **Meshtastic web client** (v2.7.2, GPL-3.0, a static `build.tar`) are S each ([Pat](https://github.com/la5nta/pat/releases/latest), [rtl_433](https://github.com/merbanan/rtl_433/releases/latest), [Meshtastic web](https://github.com/meshtastic/web/releases/latest)). Meshtastic firmware (v2.7.26) and MeshCore firmware fit the existing `embedded` pack as S recipes. **JS8Call-improved** (55–56M), **FreeDV** (65–77M), **Reticulum MeshChat** (151–154M) and gqrx are AppImage/dmg apps, so they wait on the launch convention. **Direwolf**, the key APRS/packet TNC, ships no Linux or macOS binaries, so it needs a source-build handler (M). **Nebula** (MIT, 15–32M) gives an encrypted overlay network that works on a LAN with no internet. The FCC ULS amateur licence DB (183.9 MB, public domain) is US-only and low priority. RepeaterBook and RadioReference are excluded on terms.

### cybersec: offline vulnerability intelligence

The reference side gains TED **cyber-security** (195M) and **online-privacy** (492M), the **Cloudflare Learning Center** (182M, licence unverified), **incognitocat.me** (8.9M) and **Tor Stack Exchange** (30M). The datasets fit the `nuclei-templates`/`seclists` dated-pin convention as S each: **CVE List V5** (643 MB daily zip, [cvelistV5](https://github.com/CVEProject/cvelistV5/releases/latest)), **Exploit-DB** (GPL-2.0; the GitHub mirror is archived, so use [GitLab](https://gitlab.com/exploit-database/exploitdb)), **MITRE ATT&CK STIX** (v20.1, [repo](https://github.com/mitre-attack/attack-stix-data)) and, optionally, **OSV all-ecosystems** (2.94 GB). The new tools are **yara-x** (v1.25.0, BSD-3, 8–9M), **gitleaks** (MIT, ~7M), **trivy** or **grype** (45–51M, which need a pre-fetched vulnerability DB as a separate dated dataset, M), and **Cutter**, a reverse-engineering GUI that does not need Ghidra's JDK (AppImage/dmg). ImHex and mitmproxy are larger options.

### ai: upgrade llama.cpp, then add embeddings, translation and voice

After the llama-server bump (see the fixes section), the AI gaps fall into three groups. The first is the **search-quality** pair. **EmbeddingGemma 2** (Q8_0 0.31 GB, Apache-2.0, 100+ languages including Finnish, MRL truncation to 256 dimensions) would replace the English-centric `nomic-embed-text-v1.5` ([card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2), [GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/embeddinggemma-2-GGUF)). It is an **L**: it changes the vector space and the prompt prefixes, and the search checkpoint's exact-model reader contract means every semantic index must be rebuilt. **Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B** (0.64 GB, Apache-2.0) adds a second retrieval stage through llama-server's reranking endpoint ([HF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B-Q8_0-GGUF)). It is an M, or an L if it changes the search result contract.

The second group is **language and documents**. **Hy-MT3-1.8B** (Q4_K_M 1.35 GB; 7B 5.15 GB; Apache-2.0; 102 languages, with Finnish new in v3) is the small Finnish↔English translator ([card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy-MT3-1.8B)). Its Finnish quality is unmeasured, and the "beats Microsoft Translator" claim comes from the vendor. **GLM-OCR** (MIT, 0.95 + 0.48 GB) runs in llama.cpp mtmd and fits OCR of scanned Survivor Library PDFs ([GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/GLM-OCR-GGUF)). **Learn AI Anywhere** (96M) and Stack Exchange ai, datascience and genai are the reference additions.

The third group is the **Finnish voice stack**. For speech-to-text, the options are parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3 (25 European languages including Finnish, CC BY 4.0, Q8_0 0.71–0.74 GB), whisper large-v3-turbo q5_0 (0.57 GB, MIT) or Qwen3-ASR-0.6B/1.7B (Apache-2.0, includes Finnish, ggml-org GGUFs). Piper `fi_FI-harri-medium` (60 MB) is the text-to-speech option ([parakeet](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3), [Qwen3-ASR GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-GGUF), [piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices)). This group is an **L**. whisper.cpp ships no binaries, and Piper's successor is GPL-3.0 Python-only, so the runtime choice is sherpa-onnx (Apache-2.0, static binaries for all four platforms, runs Whisper and Piper ONNX models) or llama.cpp's new TTS and mtmd paths. That choice needs a design.

New chat models are each an S recipe plus the measurement pass Svalbard ran for Gemma and Qwen (M in total). The leading candidates for the 24 GB tier are **Inkling 2 Mini** (Apache-2.0, 26.4B total and 3.4B active, vision, UD-IQ4_XS 15.73 GB, needs ≥ b12600; the card warns of weaker non-English output) and **Muse Glimmer 30B** (Apache-2.0, vision, UD-Q4_K_XL 15.88 GB). For the 16 GB tier, the coding candidate is **Mellum2.1-12B-A2.5B** (Apache-2.0, Q4_K_M 8.07 GB). For the 64 GB tier, it is **Nanbeige4.3-33B** (Apache-2.0, Q4_K_M 20.79 GB), which ranked first on an independent Terminal-Bench score but trails Qwen3.8-27B on FrontierCode ([Inkling card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini), [Muse Glimmer](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Glimmer-30B-GGUF), [Mellum GGUF](https://huggingface.co/JetBrains/Mellum2.1-12B-A2.5B-Thinking-GGUF), [gpuwerk](https://gpuwerk.com/blog/best-open-weight-coding-models/)). **Raon-1-mini** (Apache-2.0, IQ4_XS 16.21 GB) lists Pi tool calling, which matters for the `svalbard` pi provider, but it is Korean-tuned and two days old. The Ternary-Bonsai requants are not confirmed to load in llama.cpp. Except where a cited benchmark says otherwise, every claim that a model is better comes from its vendor.

### maps (global): terrain, routing and geocoding are the missing layers

The catalog has OSM basemaps and Natural Earth, but no terrain, imagery, routing or geocoding. **Mapterhorn** terrarium terrain (CC BY 4.0 per VersaTiles; Copernicus GLO-30 to z12; the planet is 355.6 GB) can be cut with the existing `go-pmtiles extract --bbox`, and the vendored MapLibre renders `raster-dem` hillshade natively ([Mapterhorn data access](https://mapterhorn.com/data-access/), [VersaTiles sources](https://versatiles.org/sources/)). It is an M, because the viewer style needs a terrain source, and the extract size for a Finland bbox has not been measured. A **world low-zoom Protomaps extract** (for example z0–8) is an S in the `osm-extract` family ([Protomaps](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads)). VersaTiles landcover (4.1 GB, ODbL plus CC BY 4.0) and GEBCO 2025 bathymetry (7.5 GB) are larger options. A Shortbread-schema basemap (VersaTiles, OpenFreeMap, Geofabrik) would need a second style, which is not justified.

Routing and geocoding have the best data size for their value of anything in this report, and they need the most runtime work. **BRouter rd5 segments** for all of Finland are about 130–180 MB (ODbL, regenerated weekly, [segments4](https://brouter.de/brouter/segments4/)). The **Photon Finland dump** is 244.4 MB (weekly, [Photon Finland](https://download1.graphhopper.com/public/europe/finland/)). The BRouter engine needs a JRE, Photon 1.3.0 needs **Java 21+**, and GraphHopper needs a JRE too. Shipping any of them is an **L** (a Java runtime dependency). There are two Java-free paths. One is to ship the rd5 files as data for phone apps such as BRouter Android and OsmAnd (S). The other is to turn the Photon JSON, MML place names (below) or GeoNames into an SQLite FTS gazetteer (M, or L when wired into map search). **GeoNames** `cities500` (13.3 MB) or `allCountries` (403 MB), CC BY 4.0, is the global gazetteer ([GeoNames](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/allCountries.zip)). Organic Maps `.mwm` data carries its own binary data licence, and the IIAB 1.9 GB world OpenMapTiles module duplicates Protomaps, so both are low priority.

### fi-survival, fi-reference, fi-maps: kapsi and SYKE are drop-in, the rest needs builders

**fi-survival.** The Interior Ministry's 2024 guide "Häiriö- ja kriisitilanteisiin varautuminen" has printable summaries in 16 languages, including Finnish, Swedish, English, Russian, Ukrainian, Estonian, Selkosuomi and three Sámi languages. The Finnish file is 1.18 MB at `https://cdn.verkkopalvelu.suomi.fi/files/varautunut-parjaa-paremmin_SM_suomi-e093f829ad4179e1b727c8f283a3e113.pdf` ([suomi.fi summaries](https://www.suomi.fi/oppaat/varautuminen/tulostettavat-ja-viittomakieliset-varautumisohjeet/varautumisohjeiden-tulostettavat-tiivistelmat)). The SPEK guide `spek_72_tuntia_varautuminen_kotona_2025_01_21_v01.pdf` is 2.94 MB ([72tuntia.fi](https://72tuntia.fi/)). Neither states a licence, and Finnish copyright §9 exempts laws and decisions, not guides. So these are **class B** until the Interior Ministry and SPEK confirm, though as public guidance they are low-risk. The filenames contain content hashes, so the recipes pin exact URLs (S each). **HSY public water posts** (5.3 KB shapefile, CC BY 4.0, updated 2026-11) are an S `vector-static` layer for the capital region ([CKAN](https://avoindata.suomi.fi/data/fi/dataset/paakaupunkiseudun-vesipostit)). Open data cannot fill two gaps: civil-defence shelter locations and Metsähallitus huts. Neither has a national open dataset. OSM tags and `lipas-recreation` are the substitutes, and whether LIPAS includes autiotupa and laavu types is worth checking. **Fineli** (Finnish food composition, CC BY 4.0 per a University of Helsinki page) is the next most valuable item, but every request to fineli.fi returned 403, so its URL and size are unconfirmed.

**fi-reference.** The Kotus downloads are all CC BY 4.0 XML ([Kotus aineistot](https://kotus.fi/kotus/kieliaineistot/aineistot-verkossa/)): Suomi–ruotsi-suursanakirja (`kaino.kotus.fi/lataa/suru.zip`, 11.9 MB), Suomen murteiden sanakirja (47.7 MB), Vanhan kirjasuomen sanakirja (18.0 MB) and Karjalan kielen sanakirja (16.7 MB). Each is an M, because each needs its own XML parser. The **Nykysuomen sanalista 2024** is a 2.9 MB TSV and is an S as a raw file. **Finlex consolidated legislation** (`statute-consolidated.zip`, 4.4 GB Akoma Ntoso XML, CC BY 4.0, refreshed daily) is an **L**. It needs a design for filtering to key acts (pelastuslaki, valmiuslaki, metsästyslaki) and an FTS build, and the output size is unmeasured ([Finlex downloads](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/avoin-data/lataa-aineistoja)). The cheap Kiwix wins are `wikipedia_fi_top_mini` (112M, a phone tier), `wikiversity_fi` (20M), `wikiquote_fi` (19M) and the `wikipedia_fi_all_mini` build (1.0G) ([wikipedia](https://download.kiwix.org/zim/wikipedia/)). Tatoeba sentences (209.8 MB, CC BY 2.0 FR, includes Finnish) and a Finnish-only kaikki.org Wiktionary extract are later options.

**fi-maps.** The kapsi and SYKE layers below are all CC BY 4.0 and keyless. The official MML OGC APIs return 401 without a key, so kapsi is the acquisition source ([kartat.kapsi.fi](https://kartat.kapsi.fi/), [SYKE Ranta10](https://avoindata.suomi.fi/data/fi/dataset/ranta10-rantaviiva-1-10-000)).

## Vehicle repair: generic primers yes, workshop manuals no

Svalbard already covers vehicle repair at the "fix and troubleshoot" level. It has the iFixit ZIM (3.57 GB, 2025-12-21), Motor Vehicle Maintenance & Repair Stack Exchange (342 MB), Bicycles Stack Exchange (499 MB), Restarters, FarOutRide (local build), Wikibooks, DIY Stack Exchange and Gutenberg LCC-T. Kiwix's English catalog has **no dedicated car, motorcycle, small-engine or bicycle repair ZIM** beyond these ([OPDS](https://library.kiwix.org/catalog/v2/entries?count=2000&lang=eng)). Wikibooks' automotive books are thin (*Automotive Systems* is 9.6 KB) and already in `wikibooks-en` ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Subject:Automotive_engineering)). What the catalog lacks is systematic manual-style content. The legal classes divide that content cleanly.

Two small UX gaps follow from the B-class path. `svalbard import` takes exactly one file (`cobra.ExactArgs(1)` in `host-cli/internal/cli/root.go`), so importing a car manual plus a mower plus a chainsaw takes three calls. Nothing groups imported files into a "Vehicles" shelf either. Folder import with a tag or shelf is an M. Right-to-repair laws oblige manufacturers to provide repair information to owners and repairers, but none of them gives third parties a right to republish it. That is why CHARM stays class C even though it is free to download. Workshop manuals for most vehicles are sold (Toyota TIS, Honda shop manuals), and I found no openly licensed small-engine repair textbook.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the catalog's frontier lies. Kiwix has largely been mined: the Stack Exchange sites researchers expected to be missing (diy, electronics, ham, outdoors, gardening, cooking, security, unix) are already in, and NOMAD's Medicine, Survival and DIY tiers are almost fully covered. The remaining content wins are cheap but small. What sets Svalbard apart now is **structured data**: drug labels, gazetteers, dictionaries, legislation and food composition. That runs into a builder that only knows one XML schema. A generic `reference-static` table mapper (L) is therefore the highest-leverage engineering change in this report. It would turn roughly ten M-sized datasets (openFDA, FDC, GeoNames, MML place names, Kotus, WordNet, HYG, ChEBI, Fineli, the NOMAD remedy tables) into S-sized recipes. It would also give the AI pack a body of searchable, citable facts instead of more model files.

Three things are worth borrowing from similar projects, and none of them is content. NOMAD's Essential/Standard/Comprehensive tiers inside each category, with a budget of under 500 MB for the Essential tier, match Svalbard's size-tiered presets, but at pack level. Its `validate-collection-urls.yml` CI job would have caught both `ready-gov` and the Gutenberg drift early ([NOMAD repo](https://github.com/Crosstalk-Solutions/project-nomad/tree/main/.github/workflows)). A HEAD-check job over every `url_pattern` is an S, and it would turn silent staleness into a failing check. The third lesson concerns licences. Kiwix's new `community/` folder ships NC and ND content (The Last Codex, Learn AI Anywhere), so recipe licence metadata needs to carry the NC flag explicitly rather than assume that anything on download.kiwix.org is freely redistributable.
